What not to share with AI

What you type is sent to the AI provider's servers. Whether it can also be used to train their AI depends on your plan, not just the tool: work and business plans generally keep your data out of training, while free and personal plans often use it unless you switch off a setting (usually called something like "improve the model"). Check your settings — and unless your organisation has approved the tool for work data, treat what you type like a public forum.

✕
Never enter client or personal information
Names, contact details, health records, Tax File Numbers, customer records — protected under the Privacy Act and your organisation's obligations. Replace real details with placeholders like [NAME].
✕
Never paste confidential contracts, financial data, or board documents
These may be stored by the provider, used to train future models, or exposed in a breach. Use internal tools for sensitive documents.
✕
Never share passwords, API keys, or system details
Passwords, tokens, internal IP addresses, and system architecture details should never appear in an AI prompt.
✕
Never share unreleased plans, M&A activity, or sensitive strategy
Commercially sensitive information — especially anything price-sensitive for listed companies — must stay internal.
~
Be cautious with internal processes and org structure
Detailed org charts, security policies, and system architecture could help an attacker target your organisation if exposed.

Writing your own prompt

Every prompt below follows the same pattern. Use it for anything that isn't in the library.

  1. Set the scene. Who you are, what's happening, and who the result is for.
  2. Give it the facts. Paste the email, notes or text you're working from. AI can't know your situation, so without facts it fills gaps with plausible guesses.
  3. Say what you want back. Format, length and tone — e.g. "3 bullet points, under 150 words, friendly".
  4. Ask it to be honest. "Ask me questions if you need more detail" and "say if you're unsure" get better answers than letting it guess.
  5. Check before you rely on it. AI can be confidently wrong — verify facts, figures, phone numbers and anything legal, medical or financial with an official source.
